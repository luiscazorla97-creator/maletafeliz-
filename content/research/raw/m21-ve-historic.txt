 Zócalo
 The vibrant heart of the port. A space full of life where the portales, the Cathedral, and the Municipal Palace converge. It is the ultimate gathering spot to enjoy danzón, marimba music, and the warmth of Veracruz locals under the shade of its traditional palm trees.

 San Juan de Ulúa
 An imposing fortress built on a reef that served as a defensive bastion, a commercial port, and one of the most feared prisons in Mexico's history. Its coral walls tell tales of pirates, invasions, and colonial legends that shaped the destiny of the nation.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Museo Naval México
 Located in the former building of the Naval Military School, this venue offers an interactive journey through Mexico's maritime history. It features more than 20 rooms exhibiting historical artifacts, scale models, and nautical technology, paying tribute to the heroes who defended the port.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Baluarte de Santiago
 The last survivor of the seven bastions that made up the city's defensive wall in the 17th century. Known as "El polvorín" (The Powder Magazine), this historical monument is a masterpiece of military architecture that today protects pre-Hispanic treasures and the walled memory of Veracruz.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Leyes de Reforma Cultural Center
 This historical site is directly linked to the Reform Laws era and the government of Benito Juárez during the Reform War (1858-1861). In these houses of the port of Veracruz, the liberal government was temporarily established, and from here fundamental laws for the separation of Church and State were promulgated, such as the Juárez Law, the Lerdo Law, and the nationalization of ecclesiastical property. The property was recently restored and converted into a cultural center dedicated to preserving the historical memory of Mexican liberalism and Juárez's stay in Veracruz. It was officially inaugurated as a cultural center in 2024.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Hugo Argüelles Veracruz Center for the Arts
 Discover CEVART, which occupies a building whose history dates back to the 18th century. Originally it was part of the walled city of Veracruz and belonged to important merchants of the port. Throughout the 19th century it had different commercial uses, including shops, a hotel, offices, and shipping agencies. In 2004 it was inaugurated as the Veracruz Center for the Arts and named after the Veracruz dramatist Hugo Argüelles. It currently functions as an important space for artistic training, workshops, exhibitions, dance, theater, and cultural activities. In 2015 it was rehabilitated and modernized to preserve the historical building and adapt it to new cultural activities.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Benito Juárez House
 This house is one of the most important historical sites related to Benito Juárez in Veracruz. During the Reform War, Juárez and part of his cabinet occupied nearby properties in the port while defending the liberal government against the conservative movement. The house was restored to function as a historical and cultural site, displaying documents, objects, and aspects of the political life of that era. It is part of the historical complex related to the Reform Laws and helps to understand the strategic role that Veracruz played as the provisional capital of Mexico during the 19th-century conflict.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Casa Principal Cultural Center
 Casa Principal is one of the oldest colonial buildings in the historic center of Veracruz. During the viceregal era it functioned as a residence and office for Spanish merchants linked to the port, taking advantage of the intense mercantile activity of the city. Over time the property had different administrative and commercial uses until it became a cultural space managed by the Veracruz Institute of Culture. Today it is mainly dedicated to contemporary art exhibitions, photography, painting, and cultural activities, preserving much of its historical architecture.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Salvador Díaz Mirón House
 This museum was the residence of the Veracruz poet and journalist Salvador Díaz Mirón, one of the most important literary figures in 19th and early 20th-century Mexico. The property preserves furniture, documents, library, and personal objects of the writer. Díaz Mirón stood out for his modernist poetry and also for his intense political and journalistic activity. The house was transformed into a museum to preserve his literary legacy and show aspects of the intellectual life of Veracruz at that time. It is currently one of the most representative cultural museums in the historic center.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Veracruz City Museum
 The City Museum of Veracruz, named in honor of Colonel Manuel Gutiérrez Zamora, is set in an old historical building in the center of the port. The site safeguards pieces, photographs, and documents related to the evolution of Veracruz from the colonial era to the present. Its rooms explain themes such as the foundation of the city, maritime trade, foreign invasions, the daily life of the port, and the urban development of the historic center. The museum also recognizes the importance of Manuel Gutiérrez Zamora, a liberal Veracruz politician and defender of the reformist ideas of the 19th century.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Faro Venustiano Carranza
 A majestic neoclassical style building inaugurated in 1910 by Porfirio Díaz to commemorate the centennial of Independence. With its iconic lighthouse and clock, this venue not only served as a marine guide but also functioned as the provisional headquarters of the Constitutionalist Government led by Venustiano Carranza in 1914. Its walls and courtyards are today a living emblem of the rich naval history and the revolutionary processes that forged contemporary Mexico.

 The Cathedral of Veracruz
 The imposing Cathedral of Our Lady of the Assumption, an icon of 17th-century faith and architecture in the heart of the port.

 Macroplaza del Malecón
 The great window to the sea of Veracruz and the social heartbeat of the entire port. This wide pedestrian esplanade invites locals and tourists to enjoy the coming and going of imposing commercial vessels and Navy ships, surrounded by the Gulf breeze. It is the perfect setting to savor a traditional ice cream, enjoy spontaneous live music, watch folkloric shows, and witness beautiful ocean sunsets.

 José María Morelos
 Statue formerly located on Miguel Alemán Avenue and moved to its current location, next to the Morelos Bridge in the historic center of the city in the renovated Morelos Plaza on July 12, 2016. The statue is made of bronze and is over 100 years old.

 Gen. Ciriaco Vázquez
 Gen. Ciriaco Vázquez died in the Battle of Cerro Gordo (1794-1847). The park that bears his name was inaugurated following the destruction of the wall in 1891.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Gen. Nicolás Bravo
 Nicolás Bravo belongs to the Porfiriato historical period. The monument to charity of the civil registry is a statue donated by the Spanish colony in 1910 for the centennial celebrations of independence in gratitude for the gesture of forgiveness granted by the General to 300 Spanish prisoners during the war of independence.

 Miguel Hidalgo
 Miguel Hidalgo was placed on May 8, 1964, in Plazuela Hidalgo; the sculptor of the work was Manuel Alpinos. In the 90s it was placed in Ciriaco Vázquez Park, where it is currently located.

 Francisco I. Madero
 Francisco I. Madero (1873-1913) was a Mexican businessman, writer, and politician known as the "Apostle of Democracy." As leader of the National Anti-Reelectionist Party, he challenged the dictatorship of Porfirio Díaz and succeeded in being elected president of Mexico in 1911, before being tragically overthrown and assassinated.

 Benito Juárez García
 In Porfirio Díaz Park, the monumental work of the column was built, counting as a pedestal for the statue of Don Benito Juárez and 2 symbolic eagles of the coat of arms, naming the complex as the Benito Juárez monument.

 La Caridad
 It represents a woman with 2 children, one of whom seeks to feed from the mother's breast, and this gesture is assumed as an act of charity, feeding the helpless. On the site where the current registry is located, there was a hostel called San Charles, which collapsed to make way for the current building inaugurated in 1972.

 Commemorative Column to Liberty
 Porfirian monument built in 1901, inaugurated by Porfirio Díaz. Column made of marble and quarry stone, 14 meters high. Located at the corner of Zaragoza and Rayón avenues, it features stamped concrete, red Porfiriato flooring, and quarries. The complex is named as the plaza and column to the homeland.

 To the Firefighters
 Firefighter, a sculpture by Humberto Peraza, symbolizes the firefighter before a disaster rescuing a child with all his equipment; it was promoted by commanders Escamilla and Limón.

 Agustín Lara
 In November 2000, the statue of the musician was placed at the entrance of what was Alameda Díaz Mirón. It is currently located in the "Toña La Negra" alley in the La Huaca neighborhood, relocated at the request of the neighbors and with authorization from INAH.

 Toña La Negra
 Work by Humberto Peraza following the tribute that imposed the name on the alley where the singer was born on November 5, 1983.

 Pope John Paul II
 To commemorate the visit made by His Holiness John Paul II to Veracruz on May 7, 1990, a bronze statue was placed in the square of the Cristo del Buen Viaje Church.

 Fray Martín de Valencia
 This piece is part of a series of sculptures created within the framework of the celebrations of the 500 years of Veracruz. It was created by sculptor Glenda Estela Hecksher Ramsden. Fray Martín de Valencia led the conquest expedition of New Spain.

 Monument to the Niños Héroes
 The monument in Zamora Park in Veracruz is dedicated to the Niños Héroes of Chapultepec, the cadets of the Heroic Military College who died during the US invasion of 1847.

 Manuel Gutiérrez Zamora
 The statue of Manuel Gutiérrez Zamora is the only physical vestige remaining of the old Alameda of Veracruz, inaugurated in September 1892. It is currently located at a corner of Zamora Park.

 Francisco J. Clavijero
 Prominent Jesuit priest, historian, and Creole philosopher born in the port of Veracruz. He is recognized as the father of Mexican historiography and a pillar of early nationalism.

 Gen. Heriberto Jara Corona
 In December 2017, months after it was removed from the airport (which it gave its name to), the statue was placed in the Grand Plaza of the Malecón of Veracruz.

 Spanish Immigrant
 The Spanish immigrant Don Juan José Venta Corrales donated the Spanish immigrant statue to the municipality of Veracruz in December 2009, which is placed on the Veracruz malecón.

 Benny Moré
 Life-size statue of Cuban musician Benny Moré, located in the Lagunilla alley. Cuba provided the effigy in reciprocity for the statue of Agustín Lara donated by Veracruz.

 Monument to the Mother
 The Monument to the Mother, inaugurated on May 10, 1949, on Insurgentes Norte Avenue, was created by sculptor Luis Ortiz Monasterio and architect José Villagrán García as a tribute to motherhood and the family. Its origin dates back to 1922, when Mother's Day was instituted in Mexico thanks to Rafael Alducín and José Vasconcelos. The sculptural group, in Art Deco style with pre-Hispanic influence, includes figures representing the mother, education, and fertility. After suffering serious damage in the earthquake of September 19, 2017, the monument was restored and reinaugurated in 2018 using original materials.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Altar to the Homeland
 The Altar to the Homeland, popularly known as the obelisk of the heroes, was inaugurated on April 21, 1998, in the roundabout of 16 de Septiembre and Arista streets in the center of Veracruz. This monument replaced an earlier one from 1952 and was created to pay tribute not only to the heroes of the US invasions of 1847 and 1914, but also to the defenders of the French intervention of 1838 and the victims of the bombardment of 1823, events linked to the city's title of "Four Times Heroic." The obelisk, covered in black marble and raised on staircases, contains inscriptions referring to each conflict and seeks to concentrate in a single site the memory of those who defended Veracruz against foreign attacks.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Monument to the Lebanese Emigrant
 The Monument to the Lebanese Emigrant, located on Manuel Ávila Camacho Boulevard in the port of Veracruz, was erected as a tribute to the Lebanese community that arrived in the city between the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Many immigrants from the then Ottoman Empire arrived at the Veracruz port seeking new commercial opportunities and a better quality of life. Over time, Lebanese families deeply integrated into the economic, cultural, and social life of Veracruz, outstanding in commerce, gastronomy, and business life. This monument symbolises gratitude for their contribution to the development of the port and keeps alive the memory of those who crossed the ocean to settle in Mexico.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Jewish Immigration Commemorative Monument
 The Jewish Immigration Commemorative Monument recalls the arrival of Jewish families who entered Mexico through the port of Veracruz during different stages of the 20th century. Many of these immigrants were escaping conflicts, persecutions, and economic crises in Europe and the Middle East, finding in Veracruz a gateway to a new life. The Jewish community contributed significantly to the cultural, educational, and commercial growth of the region, while preserving their traditions and customs. The monument represents hope, integration, and historical recognition of those immigrants who made Mexico their new home.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Tram of Remembrance
 The Tram of Remembrance is a historical and tourist monument that evokes the era when trams were an essential part of urban transport in Veracruz. During much of the 20th century, trams ran through the streets of the historic center and connected different areas of the city, becoming a daily symbol of port life. With the modernization of transport, this system disappeared, but one of the old cars was preserved as a tribute to that stage of Veracruz's urban development. Currently, the tram functions as a tangible reminder of the port's history and the transformation of its mobility and urban landscape.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Veracruz Letters
 The Veracruz Letters, located on the waterfront of the port of Veracruz, have become one of the most representative tourist and photographic spots in the city. This urban monument was installed as part of the efforts to strengthen the visual and cultural identity of the port, highlighting the pride of being veracruzano and the historical importance of the city as Mexico's maritime gateway. Its location facing the sea allows visitors to appreciate both port activity and the coastal landscape, making it a modern symbol that blends tradition, tourism, and daily life.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Heroismo Veracruzano Plaza
 The Heroismo Veracruzano Plaza is a commemorative space dedicated to remembering the different heroic deeds that marked the history of Veracruz and the country. The port was the scene of important military events, such as the defense against foreign invasions in the 19th and 20th centuries, which is why Veracruz received the title of "Four Times Heroic." The plaza seeks to pay tribute to the soldiers, sailors, and citizens who participated in these historical defenses, as well as serving as a public space for coexistence and historical memory facing the waterfront.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Reforma Theater
 The Reforma Theater is one of the most important and historical cultural buildings in Veracruz. Inaugurated in the 19th century, this venue was built during a stage of urban modernization of the port and quickly became a center for artistic, cultural, and political activities. Inside, theatrical plays, concerts, and official events of great relevance to Veracruz social life were held. Its name refers to the historical period of the Liberal Reform in Mexico, and over the years the building has been restored to preserve its architectural and cultural value as part of the historical heritage of Veracruz.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Francisco J. Clavijero Theater
 The Francisco J. Clavijero Theater is one of the most important and historical cultural venues in Veracruz, with theatrical antecedents dating back to the colonial era when the old Comedy House existed. The current building, inaugurated in 1902 during the Porfiriato, stands out for its neoclassical architecture of French influence and has been the stage for opera, theater, concerts, cinema, and historical events of the port. Throughout its history it suffered fires, name changes, and periods of restoration, until it became a cultural symbol of the historic center and took the name of the Veracruz historian Francisco Javier Clavijero.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Santo Cristo del Buen Viaje Parish
 The Santo Cristo del Buen Viaje Parish is one of the most traditional religious temples in the historic center of Veracruz. Its origin is related to the deep connection of the port with maritime activity, since for centuries sailors, travelers, and merchants went to the temple to entrust themselves before departing or to thank for their safe return. Devotion to the Santo Cristo del Buen Viaje became one of the most important religious expressions among the port community. Over time, the parish acquired historical and architectural relevance, forming part of the cultural and spiritual heritage of Veracruz.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Trigueros Building
 The Trigueros Building, located facing the Veracruz Zócalo at the intersection of Mario Molina and Zaragoza streets, is an iconic structure of great historical and architectural value in the heart of the Historic Center. Built in the 19th century by merchant and philanthropist Domingo Trigueros, it stands out for its colonial arcades and Neoclassical details, having served various administrative and civic functions over the centuries.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Atarazanas Cultural Center
 The Atarazanas Building, now the Atarazanas Cultural Center, is one of the oldest colonial structures in Veracruz, built in the 18th century behind the Santiago Bastion. Constructed with coral stone, it originally served as a naval shipyard and royal Spanish warehouse. Today it stands as a vibrant cultural venue hosting art exhibitions, workshops, and traditional events.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Patio de Vergara
 Patio de Vergara, home to the Historical Archive and Library of the City of Veracruz on Landero y Coss Street, is a pristine 18th-century colonial architectural ensemble. Formerly a prominent merchant residence and courtyard, it now preserves priceless historical documents, maps, and civic records spanning over five centuries of history.
 LEARN MORE → 
 San Francisco Convent (Benito Juárez Lighthouse)
 The former Convent of San Francisco, later known as the Benito Juárez Lighthouse and Reform Enclosure, is a paramount 16th-century historic monument. Its grand tower served as Veracruz's first lighthouse during the 19th century. Within these historic walls, President Benito Juárez enacted the monumental Reform Laws between 1859 and 1860, establishing the separation of Church and State in Mexico.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Municipal Palace (City Hall)
 The Municipal Palace of Veracruz, historically known as Casa de Cabildos, stands proudly facing the main Zócalo. It serves as the seat of the oldest municipal government in continental America, founded by Hernán Cortés in 1519. Featuring classic 17th and 18th-century neoclassical architecture, elegant arcades, and a grand central courtyard, it has been the civic epicenter of Veracruz for over five centuries.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Veracruz Institute of Culture (IVEC)
 Headquarters of the Veracruz Institute of Culture (now Veracruz Secretariat of Culture), this iconic venue occupies the former 18th-century Convent of San José de Gracia. Built with traditional coral stone and brick, the complex features lush courtyards, colonial cloisters, and art galleries, hosting cultural exhibitions, workshops, and international artistic events.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Callejón J.J. Herrera (Callejón de los Desamparados / Callejón de la Inquisición)
 Historically known as Callejón de los Desamparados and Callejón de la Inquisición, today it bears the official name of José Joaquín Herrera. This passage was used to transfer and lock up people accused of heresy, blasphemy, or sodomy before being taken to Mexico City to be tried by the Holy Inquisition. It is closely related to the current Casa Principal, where it is believed the Tribunal of the Holy Office was located. It also stands out for its ancient constructions made with hard coral and mortar.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Callejon Trigueros
 Named in honor of Don Esteban Trigueros, a prominent merchant and alderman of the port of Veracruz in the late 18th century. This picturesque historic alley stands out for its colonial layout and ancient buildings with coral stone walls.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Callejón and Plazuela de la Lagunilla
 It is the only street in Veracruz that has preserved its name for more than 400 years. In the 17th century, the Tenoya stream passed through this site, forming a small lagoon that prevented the construction of homes. After the course was modified during the construction of the wall, the place remained as a small plaza. It stands out for its architecture similar to the alleys of Havana, the statue of Benny Moré, and for having been the setting for the film Tierra Brava in 1938.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Plazuela de la Campana
 Its name comes from a bell placed by the Dominican friars to call to religious acts, since the Santo Domingo convent did not have a bell tower. In the 19th century it was also known as "La Mesura" due to the businesses dedicated to the weighing of merchandise. Currently, it is a traditional space for jarocho music and dance, where part of the old convent is still preserved and scenes from La Mujer del Puerto were filmed in 1934.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Portal de Miranda
 The Callejón or Portal de Miranda, in the historic center of Veracruz, is an emblematic colonial space built in the 18th century by the Miranda family. It stood out as an important commercial center during the viceroyalty and was built with múcara stone and brick. It currently preserves its original architecture and houses the Veracruz Photo Library, dedicated to preserving historical images of the port.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Callejón Clavijero
 The Callejón Clavijero, in the historic center of Veracruz, is an ancient passage named in honor of the historian and Jesuit Francisco Javier Clavijero. The area preserves traditional 19th-century architecture, old courtyards, and historical buildings linked to the colonial era, in addition to being close to important cultural spaces such as the Clavijero Theater.
 LEARN MORE → 
 Callejón Toña la Negra
 The La Huaca Neighborhood, considered the oldest sector in Veracruz, arose in the 17th century outside the old colonial wall as a refuge for African slaves and cemetery workers, taking its name from the Quechua term guaca, related to burial sites. Over time it established itself as an important Afro-mestizo neighborhood, where the first houses were made with wood from shipwrecked boats and later transformed into an area of traditional neighborhoods after the fall of the walls in 1880. In addition to preserving historical courtyards recognized by the INAH and a strong jarocha identity, La Huaca is famous for being the birthplace of musical figures like Agustín Lara and Toña la Negra, as well as for keeping alive gastronomic and cultural traditions such as the Festival of the Gorda and the Picada.
