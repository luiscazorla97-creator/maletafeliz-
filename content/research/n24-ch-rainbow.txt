Battery The Charleston Harbor, formed by the Cooper and Ashley Rivers, is a favorite spot for visitors to Charleston. The rock sea wall along East Battery Street provides a walking path for visitors to view historic Battery homes lining the waterfront, White Point Gardens and Fort Sumter in the distance. [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] Charleston Area Charleston is America's most beautifully preserved architectural and historic treasure, with a rich, 300-year heritage. Beautiful homes, churches and public buildings line tree-lined streets. Magnificent formal gardens grace much of the historic district, adding to the beauty of the colonial, antebellum and Victorian architecture. [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] College and Carriage Tours Recognized as the first municipal college in the United States, the College of Charleston offers 28 undergraduate degrees. Another recognized tradition is the area's carriage tours where visitors can get a glimpse of the historic district in a slow, leisurely, clip-clop pace. [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] Cypress Gardens At Cypress Gardens, visitors can paddle a flat bottom boat through a real cypress swamp or relax in the Butterfly House which features a vast array of native butterflies. [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] Eco-Tourism The Charleston area offers a variety of nature-based activities to visitors including eco-tours, turtle talks, bird watching, crabbing clinics and more. South Carolina has 22 percent of the marshes left on the East Coast of North America. This is the most productive ecosystem in the world. Capers Island, north of the Isle of Palms, contains the longest stretch of undeveloped shoreline on the East Coast. [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] Golf The sport of golf has always been a part of the ambiance of the area, as Charleston served as the site of the first golf club in America back in 1786. The area is home to over 20 courses all with spectacular views and challenging fairways. [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] Iron Work A Charleston tradition, wrought iron gates and fences can be seen throughout Charleston, especially in well-manicured gardens. Master craftsman Philip Simmons is responsible for some of Charleston's most recognizable ironworks including those found on Church Street, East Bay Street and in front of the Charleston Visitors Center on Meeting Street. [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] Meeting and Convention Facilities Meeting and Convention Facilities. Meeting and event professionals can choose from a diverse selection of meeting and convention facilities including the Charleston Area Convention Center Complex. The complex opened in August 1999 and includes 200,000 square feet of space with an 80,000 square foot exhibit hall, 25,000 square foot ballroom and 20 meeting rooms for breakouts. The North Charleston Performing Arts Center is adjacent to the convention center and offers 2,300 seats and full production capabilities. [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] Rainbow Row Located in historic downtown, this row of famous homes are private residences painted to represent Barbadian colors and traditions. [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] Shem Creek One of the most photographed areas in the Charleston area is picturesque Shem Creek, located in Mt. Pleasant. Adjacent to the Old Village, Shem Creek serves as port for the area's shrimping fleet. Seafood restaurants line both sides of the creek, serving some of the tastiest seafood in the Southeast. [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] Shopping From outdoor markets to upscale trendy boutiques, the Charleston area offers shopping options for any taste. Antique malls and art galleries line the streets in Mt. Pleasant's Old Village and in downtown Charleston's historic district while outlying area malls carry all of the hottest designer fashions. [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] Single House An architectural icon in Charleston is the Single House, a one room wide and two or more rooms deep residence. Frequently a side porch is featured, referred to as a 'piazza', giving welcome shade and adding extra room for outdoor living. All single houses have one formal opening onto the street that serves as the property's front door. [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] St. Philip's Episcopal Church Known as the lighthouse church, St. Philip's Episcopal Church's lighted steeple once help guide ships to port. In the churchyard are the graves of John C. Calhoun, Secretary of War and Vice President of the United States and Edward Rutledge, signer of the Declaration of Independence to name a few. [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] Sweetgrass Baskets Known as one of the oldest African art forms still practiced in the United States, the 300-year-old tradition of basketmaking thrives in the Charleston area. Charlestonians are proud of this rare art form and visitors from all over have an opportunity to take this piece of history home with them. The basket itself is not the only art of this technique as this exquisite craft is a family affair and takes more than a month to complete. [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] Christmas in Charleston Christmas in Charleston celebrates the holiday season with all of the history, grace and charm this historical area has to offer. "Christmas in Charleston" is a unique month and a half long holiday festival combining enticing activities from Charleston, North Charleston, Mt. Pleasant, Isle of Palms, Kiawah Island, Seabrook Island, Sullivans Island and Folly Beach. The "Christmas in Charleston" festival has been recognized with such honorable distinctions as The Southeastern Tourism Society's 2001 Top 20 Events in the southeast and has secured Charleston as a preferred holiday destination by Travel & Leisure Magazine's "Top 10 Places in the World to Spend Christmas". [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] South Carolina Aquarium See thousands of amazing aquatic animals from river otters and sharks to loggerhead turtles at Charleston's most visited attraction - the South Carolina Aquarium. Enjoy fabulous views of Charleston Harbor and participate in fun, interactive programs. Come see the newest exhibit, UFO: Unbelievable Floating Objects - a new exhibit featuring rare exotic creatures from around the world. [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] Welcoming Fountain The Pineapple has long symbolized hospitality and that is one of the characteristics the Charleston area is known for. Awarded "Most Mannerly City in America" for the past nine years by etiquette expert Marjabelle Young Stewart the Charleston area warmly welcomes more than 4 million visitors per year. [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] Architecture of Charleston Charleston has a blend of architectural styles that originated in Barbados and the West Indies and show influences from England and Holland. Many homes unique to Charleston are designed with piazzas, also known as porches, to better circulate the breeze during summer months. [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] Broad Street Broad Street Broad Street is well known for The Four Corners of Law. This intersection at the corner of Broad and Meeting Streets is named due to the representation of municipal government (Charleston City Hall), county government (Charleston County Courthouse), federal government (U.S. Courthouse) and God's Law (St. Michael's Episcopal Church). [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] Waterways Surrounded by rivers and the Atlantic Ocean, the Charleston area offers several opportunities for visitors to "get out and sea" the sites. Take a sunset sail or river taxi across the Cooper River. Or catch a glimpse of history as you take a river cruise to Fort Sumter, the Union-occupied fort that was the target of the first shots of the Civil War. [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] Area Beaches Miles of pristine beaches surround the Charleston area, including the area of Sullivan's Island, Isle of Palms, Dewees, and Folly Beach. Year round, visitors can enjoy the area's beautiful weather, water sports, outdoor activities and more. [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] Charleston Area Gardens The Charleston area is blessed with an abundant amount of spectacular gardens. Magnolia Plantation and Its Gardens is not only the oldest major public gardens in America, it is also America's oldest man-made attraction, having been open to the public since the late 1860's. [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] Kiawah Island and surrounding areas A 10-mile stretch of undisturbed Atlantic beach, Kiawah Island abounds in natural beauty from endless acres of marsh to thick forests of pines, magnolias and massive live oaks. Neighboring Seabrook Island includes three and a half miles of pristine beach along the Atlantic shore and the banks of the Edisto River. Surfs up at Folly Beach, located just north of Kiawah Island. The island is filled with natural beauty, rare birds, history, fishing and surfing. [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] Middleton Place A carefully preserved 18th century plantation and a National Historic Landmark, Middleton Place encompasses America's oldest landscaped Gardens. Located on the banks of the Ashley River, this carefully preserved plantation has survived revolution, civil wars and an earthquake. [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] Drayton Hall Construction of the Drayton Hall plantation house began in 1738 and its Georgian-Palladian architecture represents the oldest surviving example of its kind in the American South (no plumbing or electricity was ever used). Professional guides enlighten visitors about the dynamics affecting Drayton Hall over time and lead dedicated program discussions about African American history. [ Low-Res ] [ Hi-Res ] Cuisine Charleston's distinctive regional cuisine is being celebrated throughout the country. Once thought of as a lost art, Charleston "receipts" have been resurrected, preserved and artfully woven with influences from around the world. The blending of local ingredients such as Carolina Gold rice, grits, she crab soup with ideas from abroad make Lowcountry cuisine an important component of the Charleston experience.
