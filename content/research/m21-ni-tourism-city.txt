Visit Nice
Between authenticity and urban effervescence
Whether you’re looking for a city break, a cultural break, a seaside break or a gourmet experience, indeed, you’ll find it all here in an instant. An international airport links the city to the rest of the world, and a hotel network of around 200 establishments caters for every style of traveller. By the sea, 7 km of beaches line the Côte d’Azur coastline, while a wide range of activities and major cultural and sporting events punctuate the year. In the heart of Nice, the coulée verte extends the promenade into a green setting, while opening onto a city centre bustling with shops.

A city of history and light
Since 2021, Nice has been listed as a 
UNESCO World Heritage
 Site as a 
“Riviera Winter Resort”.
 This distinction recognises the exceptional heritage of a city shaped by winter visitors who came to admire its light and enjoy its climate.
Take a stroll through the districts that gave birth to this art of living: from the 
Musiciens district
 to 
Cimiez
, let yourself be seduced by the architectural richness of its facades, the reinvented 
Gare du Sud station
 or the majestic 
Russian Orthodox Cathedral of Saint-Nicolas
.
A strong cultural identity in Nice
As the capital of the Côte d’Azur, Nice is a destination for art and culture. Its museums, galleries, architecture and festivals make it a veritable open-air museum.
Stroll through 
Vieux-Nice
, thrill to the 
Promenade des Anglais
, push open the door of the 
Musée Matisse
 or the 
Musée d’Art Contemporain
, and let yourself be inspired by the city’s creative energy.

A responsible way of life
Nice is also committed to sustainable development, confirming its role as a driving force within the Nice Côte d’Azur metropolitan area. Soft mobility, accredited accommodation, the creation of green spaces and slow tourism are all part of its daily routine.
The city’s many gardens, parks and vantage points offer a breath of fresh air in the heart of the city: 
the Promenade du Paillon

Cimiez

Mont Boron

FAQ 
 Why is Nice called Nice?
There are two main hypotheses regarding the origin of the name Nice:
The first dates back to the 4th century BC, when the Phocaeans, a people from Greece, sought to expand their presence after founding Massalia (Marseille). They settled in what is now known as the Old Town of Nice and founded Nikaia (also spelled Nikéa or Nikê). In Greek, this name means “victory.”
Historian Luc Thévenon, however, believes that the name Nice may have Ligurian origins. In that language, it means “a spring” or “a fountain,” referring to the waters of the sea.
 When did the city of Nice become French?
Nice officially became part of France in 1860, following the annexation of the County of Nice under the Treaty of Turin on 24 March 1860, confirmed by a popular vote held on 15 and 16 April 1860.
 What can I do in Nice in 3 days?
In three days in Nice, you can explore the Cours Saleya, visit the Matisse Museum, join the guided tour “When Nice Invented the Riviera,” and taste local specialities such as socca and pan bagnat.

.
 What can I see and do in Old Nice?
During your visit to Old Nice, we recommend exploring the Cours Saleya Flower Market, discovering Place Rossetti and its fountain, or joining a guided tour of Old Nice offered by the Nice Heritage Centre.
 Where can I swim in Nice?
You can swim at the beaches along the Promenade des Anglais or at La Réserve.
On the Promenade des Anglais, Lenval Beach is open to dogs.
You will also find many private beaches offering sunbed rentals, restaurants and other services.
 How do I get to Castle Hill in Nice?
You can reach Castle Hill on foot via the Lesage stairs, the streets of Old Nice, or from the port via Rue Ségurane. There is also a free lift located near Rue des Ponchettes.
 Are there any casinos in Nice, France?
Yes, there are two casinos in Nice: Casino Barrière Le Ruhl, located at 1 Promenade des Anglais, and Casino Partouche at the Palais de la Méditerranée, located at 15 Promenade des Anglais.
 Where is Nice-Saint-Augustin train station located?
Nice-Saint-Augustin train station is located in the Arenas district, in the western part of Nice, in the immediate vicinity of Nice Côte d’Azur Airport.
