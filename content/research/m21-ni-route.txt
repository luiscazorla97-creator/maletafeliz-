What to do in Nice in 1, 2 or 3 days? - Page 3
Accueil Explore Towns & Villages Coastal area Nice What to do in Nice in 1, 2 or 3 days?
Day 1: Nice, between sea, old town and first sensations
To immerse yourself in Nice in a single day is first and foremost to surrender to the dazzling colours of its Baie des Anges. The blue of the sky, the yellow-orange of the rising sun to the east of the bay, the turquoise of the calm waters of the Mediterranean, the immaculate white of the pebbles on its beaches. And it’s quite naturally on the 
Promenade des Anglais
 that your walk begins in the early hours of the morning. Why not stroll past the legendary art-deco façade of 
the Hôtel Negresco
, the city’s iconic luxury hotel?
Then head east along the Prom towards 
Rauba Capeu and Port Lympia
. Past the 
Palais de la Méditerranée and the Théâtre de Verdure, then the south-facing façade of the Opera House
, you’ll find yourself walking along 
Les Ponchettes, 
a series of narrow little houses overlooked by a promenade-terrace that was very popular with winter visitors in the 19th century. Bars and restaurants welcome you here, while a little further on, elegantly renovated old fishermen’s houses catch your eye with their balconies plunging into the Grande Bleue.
It’s time to climb 
the castle hill
 (by the stairs… or the lift), to enjoy the spectacular panorama over the town and the port, to get lost in the maze of paths drowned in greenery, and not forgetting to stop off in front of the huge waterfall.

The old town is then reached via a series of staircases and alleyways. Head for the 
Cours Saleya
 and its flower market, which you can stroll around with a slice of socca in your hand. Time for lunch! Choose 
a restaurant with the Cuisine Nissarde label
 and feast on delicious local specialities.

To start the afternoon, head to 
Place Rossetti, in the heart of Vieux-Nice,
 to visit the magnificent, baroque 
cathedral of Sainte-Raparate,
 not forgetting to treat yourself to a delicious home-made ice-cream in one of the many stalls adjoining the square. Lose yourself in the narrow streets of Vieux-Nice until you find 
Place Garibaldi
 and its superb trompe l’oeil. You can then reach 
the
Place Masséna
 by strolling along 
the Promenade du Paillon
, the city’s green lung. In the square, admire the perfect harmony between the pastel-coloured façades and the modernism of 
Conversation
, the aerial work by Catalan artist Jaume Plensa. After a snack or refreshment on the terrace of one of the square’s cafés, it’s time to indulge in some 
shopping in the Carré d’Or boutiques
 or on the majestic 
Avenue Jean-Médecin,
 starting with the famous 
Galeries Lafayette
 before making your way to 
Nicetoile
. The day can end where it began, by the sea. And why not enjoy a romantic moonlit dinner on one of the many private beaches along the Promenade des Anglais?

Day 2: Nice’s heritage, from the port to the Cimiez gardens
If you’re able to spend at least two days in Nice, take the Nice in 24 hours programme for your first day… and let us guide you! To start day 2, head for the 
Port Lympia
. Start your stroll by the 
Quai Infernet
 and the city’s spectacular Monument aux Morts. Then follow the 
Quai Lunel
, where you can visit the local fishermen and their stalls selling fresh fish from the day’s catch. Past the 
Quai Papacino
 (with its luxury yachts) and the 
Quai Cassini
 (take a look at the Church of the Port) – in summer, you can cross the harbour on board Lou Passagin (free of charge) – let yourself be charmed, on the opposite side of the basin, by the shimmering colours of the Pointus, the small wooden boats that are the pride of Nice. On the way back, admire the multicoloured facades of the buildings surrounding the port. Stop off in the antiques district.
Head for 
Rauba Capeu
 to return to the Promenade des Anglais. Take in the panoramic view over the city, and don’t forget the souvenir photo against the backdrop of the Baie des Anges, the most photographed spot in the city! 
You’re in the UNESCO area.
 To find out more, stop off at the 
World Heritage Centre
 at Les Ponchettes and take a trip back to the end of the 19th century, when aristocrats from all over Europe took up winter residence in one of the Belle Epoque hotels on the Promenade des Anglais. You can also join the “Quand Nice invente la Riviera” guided tour, offered by the Centre du Patrimoine de Nice, and explore the UNESCO-listed districts that shaped the history of the French Riviera as a renowned winter resort. 
Palais de la Méditerranée, Hôtel Westminster, Le Negresco,
 so many legendary establishments, most of which have been awarded the 
Nice Historic Hotel
 label.
To round off your morning, make sure you visit the 
Masséna Museum
, dedicated to the history and art of the city from the 18th century to the Belle Époque. Time for a bite to eat. Have lunch on a private beach, or enjoy a slice of pissaladière or a succulent pan bagnat on the pebbles…

Then take a step back in time. Head for the elegant 
Cimiez district
, where you’ll discover 
the former Hotel Regina
, specially built for Queen Victoria, followed by 
a visit to the Monastery and its gardens
. Next stop is the 
Nice Archaeological Museum
, with a stroll through the remains of the Roman arena, before finishing the afternoon with a 
visit to the Matisse Museum or the Marc Chagall National Museum
, both located in the district.
At aperitif time, climb onto one of the 
city’
s many 
rooftops
 to admire the sunset. Why not continue the evening in one of the restaurants in the Garibaldi district, then in the festive atmosphere of rue Bonaparte and Place du Pin, the Mecca of Nice nightlife.

Day 3: sea air and a break in the vineyards
Visiting Nice in three days means enjoying the pleasure of wandering around as you please, while soaking up its unique atmosphere, where the charm of tradition blends harmoniously with the modernism of this great metropolis. To the programme for the first two days, why not add on day 3 
a sea trip on a coastal boat to Villefranche-sur-Mer
. If you prefer to walk, take 
the sublime coastal path linking the Cap de Nice to the divine harbour of Villefranche
, where the turquoise waters compete with the shimmering colours of the houses of this town with its feet in the water. On your return (on foot, by bus or by train), treat yourself to a lunch of fish or shellfish at the Port Lympia to enjoy the bustle of this ever-changing district.
In the afternoon, if you have a car, head off to 
discover a wine estate on the Bellet hill 
and taste one of the delicious vintages of this sun-drenched PDO. On your return to town, take advantage of the many shops and 
stroll through the pedestrian zone
, where you can dine and enjoy the cosmopolitan atmosphere of the area.

You can, of course, personalise this 3rd day to suit your own wishes.
 For more authenticity:
 the Libération market, the former Gare du Sud station and its listed façade await you. 
 For the more sporty and nature lovers:
 take a walk to Cap de Nice and climb Mont Boron, where you’ll enjoy a sublime view of Nice and the harbour of Villefranche-sur-Mer. 
 For culture and heritage buffs: 
opt for one of the guided tours offered by the Centre du Patrimoine, discover the Palais Lascaris in Vieux-Nice, climb to the top of the Tour Saint-François, or delve into the heart of Nice’s Baroque heritage. 
 For families:
 the Phoenix Park is a must, with its lush greenhouses and numerous animals. For an even more adventurous experience, there’s a rope park for children aged 3 and over, as well as a thrill-seeking park for older children.
